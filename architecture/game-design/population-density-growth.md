# Population Density & Growth

- **Residents per tile by zone type and density tier**:

| Zone        | Density | Residents/tile |
| ----------- | ------- | -------------- |
| Residential | Low     | 100            |
| Residential | Medium  | 400            |
| Residential | High    | 1,000          |
| Commercial  | Low     | 25 workers     |
| Commercial  | Medium  | 100 workers    |
| Commercial  | High    | 300 workers    |
| Industrial  | Low     | 50 workers     |
| Industrial  | Medium  | 200 workers    |
| Industrial  | High    | 600 workers    |

**Calibration note**: These values are aligned with the Economy Model calibration (see [Economy Model](economy-model.md)). At R-Low with 100 residents/tile, 10% tax rate, and $50 base income: `100 × $50 × 0.10 = $500/fully-occupied tile`; at 50% occupancy = $250/tile. C-Low: 25 workers × $50 × 0.10 = $125/fully-occupied tile; at 50% occupancy = $62.50/tile. I-Low: 50 workers × $50 × 0.10 = $250/fully-occupied tile; at 50% occupancy = $125/tile. A standard opening layout of 20 R-Low / 10 C-Low / 5 I-Low tiles at 50% occupancy produces approximately $6,400–$8,000 monthly revenue from tax plus utility fees, reaching the $8–12K target at higher occupancy rates during the bootstrap demand period. See [Economy Model](economy-model.md) for the full calibration derivation. These capacities must remain synchronized with the economy model calibration; if either changes, both must be updated together.

- **Population growth tick rate**: Population updates every budget tick (1 in-game month). Growth is not continuous. **Bootstrap note**: During the first 6 budget ticks (ticks 0–5), demand factors receive a startup subsidy — see [Zoning System](zoning-system.md) for the bootstrap mechanism that prevents zero-demand deadlock on an empty map.
- **Desirability in growth formula**: The `desirability` value used in the growth formula ranges from 0 to 100. A newly zoned tile starts at the base value of **50** (neutral) as defined in [Zoning System](zoning-system.md). Adjacency effects, service coverage, and terrain modifiers shift this value up or down within [0, 100].
- **Growth formula per tile**: `actual_population = max_density_for_tier × demand_factor × (desirability / 100)`. Growth toward the target is capped at `+10% of max_density per tick` to prevent instant population spikes. **Decay cap**: population may not decrease by more than **15% of `max_density_for_tier` per budget tick** (slightly larger than growth cap to create urgency — population falls faster than it rises, incentivizing prompt infrastructure investment). A tile where demand_factor and desirability both drop to zero targets a population of 0, but reaches it gradually over multiple ticks rather than collapsing in a single budget cycle.
- **Demand-to-density gating**: Unlock thresholds (Normal difficulty baseline; scaled by `SimulationConstants::density_unlock_scale_easy` (0.70×), `density_unlock_scale_normal` (1.00×), or `density_unlock_scale_hard` (1.50×) per difficulty — three separate fields per `architecture/game-design/economy-model.md` (a single field cannot hold per-difficulty values)): Medium-density Residential and Commercial require Monthly Revenue ≥ $50,000 for 3 consecutive months. Medium-density Industrial requires Monthly Revenue ≥ $75,000 for 3 consecutive months. High-density Residential requires Monthly Revenue ≥ $100,000 for 3 consecutive months **and** Medium-Industrial must already be unlocked (hard prerequisite gate — see [Economy Model](economy-model.md) for the supply-chain rationale and simultaneous-threshold ordering rules). High-density Commercial requires Monthly Revenue ≥ $200,000 for 3 consecutive months. High-density Industrial requires Monthly Revenue ≥ $500,000 for 3 consecutive months. Tiles only auto-upgrade density when the zone-level demand factor for that zone type ≥ `SimulationConstants::density_upgrade_wave_demand_threshold` (0.50) and the appropriate density tier has been unlocked. **V1 note:** `architecture/game-design/zoning-system.md` documents a per-tile upgrade eligibility threshold of 0.75 (a stricter per-tile concept, distinct from the zone-level wave gate); this per-tile threshold is a design concept deferred to post-V1 and is not currently implemented as a code constant or check in `Population::scanUnlockCandidates()`. The number of tiles that may upgrade in a single budget tick scales linearly with demand: `rate = ((demand − threshold) / (1.0 − threshold)) × density_max_upgrade_rate_per_tick`, clamped to a minimum of 1 tile. At demand = 0.50 (threshold) the wave fires with rate ≈ 0 (1-tile trickle); at demand = 1.0 the full 20% rate applies. [Economy Model](economy-model.md) is the authoritative source for all unlock thresholds, prerequisite gates, and difficulty scaling.
- **Demand factor calculation** (`demand_factor` per zone type, in [0.0, 1.0]):
  **Canonical authority**: [Zoning System](zoning-system.md) is the authoritative source for all demand formulas. The formulas below are summaries for context; the full definitions (including constants and design rationale) are in `zoning-system.md`. If these summaries ever conflict with `zoning-system.md`, `zoning-system.md` takes precedence.
  - **Residential demand**: `R_demand = clamp(total_C_I_worker_capacity / max(1, current_R_population), 0.0, 1.0)`. Residential demand is driven by total job capacity — residents move to cities where jobs exist. A city with more C/I worker slots than current residential capacity has R_demand = 1.0 (full demand). A city with no C/I zones has R_demand = 0 (no jobs → no population incentive). **This is the Industrial→Residential feedback loop**: placing I/C zones directly increases R_demand, pulling residential population growth. **Do NOT use unfilled-slot variants** of this formula — see `zoning-system.md` for the authoritative explanation.
  - **Commercial demand**: `C_demand = clamp(current_R_population / total_C_worker_capacity, 0.0, 1.0)`. Commercial demand is driven by the resident consumer base. More residential population → more demand for commercial services. A city with large residential population but few C zones has C_demand = 1.0 (undersupplied). **This is the Residential→Commercial feedback loop**: growing residential population directly increases C zone demand.
  - **Industrial demand**: Driven by two independent sources — Residential raw-material demand and Commercial goods consumption. See [Zoning System](zoning-system.md) for the authoritative formula and constants (`R_raw_material_rate`, `C_goods_consumption_rate`). High-level: `I_demand = clamp((R_raw_material_demand + C_goods_demand) / I_production_capacity, 0.0, 1.0)`. The two-source design ensures Industrial zones are viable even before Commercial zones are placed, preventing a zero-demand deadlock at game start.
  - All three demand factors feed back into each other over successive budget ticks, creating a stable equilibrium when zone balance is maintained. An imbalanced city (e.g., all R, no C/I) has low R_demand and poor R growth despite having residential zones zoned.
  - **Design note**: The three-way feedback (I→R→C→I) means players must balance all three zone types to grow the city. Overinvesting in any one zone type lowers the demand factor for that type, slowing growth naturally without requiring hard caps.
- **Growth and decay caps are relative to `max_density_for_tier`** (not `actual_population`):
  - Growth cap: `+10% of max_density_for_tier` per budget tick. A Low-density residential tile (max 100 residents) may gain at most 10 residents per tick regardless of demand or desirability.
  - Decay cap: `−15% of max_density_for_tier` per budget tick. A Low-density residential tile may lose at most 15 residents per tick. Using `max_density_for_tier` (not `actual_population`) ensures the cap is stable and not arbitrarily small when the tile is nearly empty.

## SimulationConstants Mapping

The following named constants in `simulation_constants.h` canonicalize values that appear as inline literals ("+10%", "−15%") in this spec. Phase 6 property-based tests MUST reference these names rather than hardcoding magic numbers. Any change to a value requires updating the constant definition only.

| Constant name                                         | Value   | Spec meaning                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `SimulationConstants::population_growth_cap_fraction` | `0.10f` | Maximum population growth per budget tick, expressed as a fraction of `max_density_for_tier`. Concretely: `growth_cap = max_density_for_tier × population_growth_cap_fraction`. For a Low-density Residential tile (max 100) this caps growth at 10 residents/tick.                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `SimulationConstants::population_decay_cap_fraction`  | `0.15f` | Maximum population decay per budget tick, expressed as a fraction of `max_density_for_tier`. Concretely: `decay_cap = max_density_for_tier × population_decay_cap_fraction`. For a Low-density Residential tile (max 100) this caps decay at 15 residents/tick. The decay cap is intentionally larger than the growth cap to create asymmetric urgency — populations fall faster than they rise, incentivizing prompt infrastructure investment. |
| `SimulationConstants::density_upgrade_wave_demand_threshold` | `0.50f` | Gate: upgrade wave does not fire when demand is below this value. Also the lower bound for the demand-scaled rate formula (excess = 0 at this value). |
| `SimulationConstants::density_max_upgrade_rate_per_tick`     | `0.20f` | Maximum fraction of zone tiles that may upgrade in one budget tick; reached only when demand == 1.0. At demand == threshold the effective rate approaches 0 (min-clamped to 1 tile). |

**Implementation note**: Both constants are `float`. They are applied as multipliers against `max_density_for_tier` (an integer per-tier value), yielding a floating-point delta that is then converted to an integer resident count via `static_cast<int>(std::round(...))` (symmetrical round-half-away-from-zero) before application. This rounding rule applies to both the growth cap and the decay cap. Phase 6 property-based tests must use this identical rounding semantic — `static_cast<int>(std::round(max_density_for_tier * fraction))` — when computing expected cap values; any divergence from this form (e.g., truncation via `static_cast<int>(...)` without `std::round`, or ceiling/floor rounding) constitutes a spec violation. Both must be defined as `static constexpr float` members of `SimulationConstants` in `simulation_constants.h`.
